Overlays are the operating system's own furniture — the status bar, the gesture bar, a menu bar and dock. They are drawn on top of your screenshot, so you do not need to include them in the capture.

Each is independently switchable, and only the ones that suit the selected device are offered. A phone is never offered a macOS dock; a laptop is never offered a gesture bar.

## Phone status bar

| Control | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Status bar | The whole strip on or off |
| Time | Any text. Defaults to 9:41 |
| Carrier | Shown before the time. Blank hides it |
| Dark glyphs | For light app content |
| Signal / Wi-Fi / Battery | Each icon individually |
| Charge | Battery fill level |

## Navigation

**Gesture bar** is the home indicator. **Nav buttons** is the three-button Android alternative — they occupy the same space, so turning on nav buttons replaces the gesture bar.

**Dark bar** switches the indicator to dark, for light app content.

## Desktop

Clamshells and desktops get a **menu bar** and a **dock** instead, with a control for how many dock items to show. **Light desktop UI** switches both to a light appearance.

## Why they are overlays

They are drawn as a transparent layer in front of the screen rather than built into the device. That means they work identically on a procedural device and on a 3D model you imported, and it means turning one off costs nothing.
