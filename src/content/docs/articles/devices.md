Every device is generated from a data file describing its real dimensions in millimetres — not loaded from a 3D model. That is what makes its colour, finish and details live controls rather than baked-in pixels.

## Colour

The **Device** tab offers three ways to colour a device:

- **Colourways** — the finishes the real device ships in, as swatches.
- **Paint** — a free palette, for matching a brand rather than a product.
- **Body and Frame** — exact hex values, if you know what you want.

Painting a device derives the rail colour automatically by lifting the body colour toward white, which is what a brushed metal band actually does next to a glass back.

## Materials

Under **Materials**, the frame and the back can each take any finish:

| Finish | Character |
| --- | --- |
| Brushed titanium / aluminium / steel | Directional grain, streaked highlights |
| Anodised aluminium | Metal with no grain, softer |
| Polished metal | Mirror-like |
| Matte glass | Soft, diffused reflections |
| Glossy glass | Sharp reflections |
| Ceramic | Between matte glass and plastic |
| Soft-touch / glossy plastic | Non-metallic, warmer |

**As built** means "however this device really is", which is the default.

The screen has its own axis — **glossy** mirrors the room, **matte** scatters it. This is the most noticeable material choice on a device, because the screen is the largest flat surface facing the camera.

## Details

Each physical feature can be switched off independently: the notch or Dynamic Island, the camera bump, the side buttons, the metal frame. Only the ones a device actually has are offered.

## Placement

**Turn**, **Tilt** and **Roll** orient the device; **Landscape** rotates it a quarter turn; **Levitate** lifts it off the plinth for a floating hero shot.
