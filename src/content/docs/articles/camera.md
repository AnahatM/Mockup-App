The camera decides the composition. There are nine angle presets, full manual control, and two navigation modes.

## Angle presets

| Preset | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Front | Straight on. Store listings, comparisons |
| Hero | Slight turn and lift. The default |
| Three-quarter | Turned further, shows depth |
| Low hero | From below, makes the product look larger |
| Top down | Almost overhead |
| Floating | Turned the other way, higher, wider |
| Dutch | Sharply angled, energetic |
| Macro detail | Very close, tight lens |
| Profile | Side on, shows thickness |

Presets are spherical offsets rather than fixed positions, so the same nine work correctly on a watch and on a 27" monitor without any per-device tuning.

## Field of view

Lower values are more telephoto and flatten perspective — closer to how product photography is actually shot. Higher values exaggerate depth and can make a device look distorted. Around 28–34 is a good range.

## Navigation

**Orbit** circles a target. Drag to turn, right-drag or middle-drag to pan, scroll to zoom. Speeds for each are adjustable, along with damping.

**Fly** releases the camera entirely: **W A S D** to move, **R** and **F** for height, drag to look. Useful for getting behind or inside a scene, which orbiting around a fixed point cannot do.

## Framing

**Frame device** — or the **F** key — recomposes the shot around whatever device is selected. It is the fastest way back when you have lost the product off-screen.

**Look at** moves the point the camera orbits, if you want to centre on part of a device rather than all of it.

## Auto rotate

Spins the camera slowly around the product. This is a viewport convenience; for a recorded video use a motion clip instead, which loops cleanly. See [Recording video](/docs/recording-video).
